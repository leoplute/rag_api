AJ DYBANTSA: THE JUMBO WING AND CONSENSUS FRONT-RUNNER
To understand the allure of AJ Dybantsa, one must examine a freshman season at Brigham Young University (BYU) that shattered historical records for one-and-done prospects. Choosing to play in Provo under the guidance of head coach Kevin Young--who left the NBA coaching ranks specifically to build an elite, modern pro-style program at BYU--Dybantsa completely vindicated the immense hype that followed him since he reclassified into the recruiting class of 2025. Standing at a legitimate 6-foot-9 with an elastic body type, an explosive vertical leap, and a wingspan stretching well past 7 feet, Dybantsa possesses the absolute ideal physical archetype for a modern NBA perimeter alpha.  
CBS Sports
Statistically, Dybantsa's lone season with the Cougars was nothing short of breathtaking. He led the entire nation in scoring, averaging an astonishing 25.5 points, 6.8 rebounds, and 3.7 assists per game, while maintaining an incredibly efficient 51.0 percent shooting accuracy from the floor. His campaign was punctuated by iconic, viral performances that demonstrated an elite capacity to perform under maximum pressure. In his Big 12 Tournament debut, Dybantsa exploded for 40 points in a commanding victory over Kansas State, breaking Kevin Durant's longstanding record for the most points scored by a freshman in the history of the tournament. He followed this up in the NCAA Tournament against the Texas Longhorns, where despite a hard-fought first-round exit, he recorded a monumental double-double with 35 points and 10 rebounds--the third-highest scoring output by a freshman in tournament history.  
CBS Sports
Dybantsa swept the national awards, earning consensus First-Team All-American honors, the Big 12 Freshman of the Year award, and the prestigious Julius Erving Award given to the nation's top small forward. On the court, Dybantsa projects as a devastating three-level scorer. He can create his own shot effortlessly off the bounce, pull up over defenders with an unblockable release point, and finish with thunderous, high-flying authority at the rim. Scouts have also noted significant, rapid development in his live-dribble playmaking and willingness to absorb and finish through contact. Defensively, his lateral quickness and immense length give him the ceiling of an elite, multi-positional lockdown defender. His common NBA player comparisons range from the smooth scoring and two-way dominance of Paul George to the explosive, unguardable wing mechanics of a young Tracy McGrady.

CC_002: DARRYN PETERSON: THE PURE SHOT-MAKER AND SCORING DYNAMO
While Dybantsa captured the national spotlight with his historic scoring volume, Darryn Peterson remains an equally mesmerizing, hyper-talented prospect who enters the pre-draft process with an immense chip on his shoulder. Standing at 6-foot-6 with broad shoulders, a powerful upper body, and an exceptionally advanced handle, Peterson spent his freshman season playing under Hall of Fame coach Bill Self for the Kansas Jayhawks.  
Sports Illustrated
Peterson's statistical output at Kansas was elite, averaging 20.2 points, 4.2 rebounds, 1.6 assists, and 1.4 steals per game, while registering an impressive shooting line of 43.8 percent from the field, 38.2 percent from beyond the three-point arc, and 82.6 percent from the free-throw line. These numbers earned him an All-Big 12 Second Team selection and an Honorable Mention AP All-American nod. However, Peterson's lone collegiate season was heavily disrupted by health issues, which caused him to miss 11 games and prevented him from cementing himself as the undisputed number-one pick. He battled a lingering hamstring injury, an ankle sprain, and most notoriously, severe bouts of full-body cramping that frequently forced him to exit the court during critical second halves and led to a brief emergency room hospitalization.  
247 Sports
At the NBA Draft Combine in mid-May 2026, Peterson and his camp successfully cleared the air regarding these medical concerns. Peterson revealed that the debilitating cramps were a direct result of improper, excessively high doses of creatine supplements he had taken prior to the season--a mistake that has since been entirely corrected under professional medical supervision. With his health fully vindicated, talent evaluators have refocused on his basketball superpower: his profound speed and unshakeable shot-making ability. Described by ESPN draft analysts as the most gifted pure scorer and natural bucket-getter in the entire 2026 class, Peterson is an absolute maestro in isolation and pick-and-roll scenarios. He can manipulate professional-level defenders with subtle changes of pace, possesses a lethal mid-range turnaround jumper, and hits deep three-pointers with effortless mechanics. While some critics questioned his low assist numbers and occasional passivity at Kansas, scouts widely believe that Bill Self's rigid, high-low offensive system suppressed Peterson's true capabilities as a dynamic, on-ball creator and secondary playmaker. In an open, space-heavy NBA offense, Peterson projects as an unstoppable, high-volume combo guard capable of anchoring an entire team's perimeter offense.

THE FIT WITH THE WASHINGTON WIZARDS: A CORNERSTONE FOR THE CAPITAL
For the Washington Wizards, a franchise that has been patiently undergoing a comprehensive, ground-up rebuilding process, landing either Dybantsa or Peterson would provide the definitive, blue-chip centerpiece the organization has desperately lacked since trading Bradley Beal. The Wizards' current roster is a collection of intriguing young pieces and developmental prospects, but it severely lacks a true, high-ceiling primary option who can demand double-teams and dictate the geometry of the court.
If the Wizards select AJ Dybantsa, he would immediately step into the starting lineup as the team's foundational franchise wing. Dybantsa's size, length, and transition scoring would complement Washington's existing young backcourt pieces, giving them an elite target on the wing who can generate offense completely out of nothing when the half-court system bogs down. Dybantsa's modern, defensive versatility would also give the Wizards a legitimate multi-positional defender to anchor their perimeter defense, allowing them to construct a lengthy, modern lineup that aligns with the contemporary standards of Eastern Conference contenders like the Boston Celtics and Orlando Magic.
Conversely, if the Wizards opt for Darryn Peterson, they would be injecting a lethal, dynamic perimeter engine into their backcourt. Peterson's elite isolation scoring and pick-and-roll mastery would give Washington a high-volume guard who can command the ball and ease the playmaking and scoring burdens on their younger players. Peterson's exceptional three-point shooting (38.2 percent at Kansas) would provide immediate floor spacing, and his ability to manufacture efficient shots late in the shot clock would give the Wizards an immediate tactical identity. Choosing Peterson would signify that Washington believes his elite speed and shot-making translate into a higher offensive peak than Dybantsa, giving them a definitive, multi-level scoring guard around whom they can build a top-tier offense.

THE FIT WITH THE UTAH JAZZ: DANNY AINGE'S MASTER PLAN
The evaluation of these two prospects takes on a fascinating, highly nuanced dynamic when viewed through the lens of the Utah Jazz. Operating with a massive war chest of future draft picks and young assets, Jazz CEO Danny Ainge has been calculating and patient in his rebuild, waiting for the precise moment to strike and secure a true, superstar-level cornerstone.
The local connection between AJ Dybantsa and the state of Utah adds a massive layer of intrigue to this scenario. Having played his college basketball just 45 miles south of Salt Lake City at BYU, Dybantsa is already a massive, beloved cultural phenomenon along the Wasatch Front. The Jazz fan base is intimately familiar with his historic 40-point performances and his collegiate dominance. Beyond the marketing and regional synergy, Dybantsa's on-court fit with the Jazz is seamless. Pairing a 6-foot-9 jumbo wing with Utah's existing All-Star forward Lauri Markkanen would give the Jazz an incredibly lengthy, skilled, and versatile frontcourt that would terrorize opposing defenses. Dybantsa's shot creation would alleviate pressure on young guard Keyonte George, allowing George to operate as a dynamic playmaker rather than being forced to carry an overwhelming scoring load. Furthermore, Dybantsa's high basketball IQ and adaptive passing would thrive within head coach Will Hardy's fluid, movement-heavy offensive system.

Should the Jazz select Darryn Peterson, Ainge would be acquiring a player whose raw competitive edge and elite scoring profile align perfectly with the historic lineage of legendary Jazz guards. Peterson's ability to explode out of pick-and-roll actions and separate from defenders with pure acceleration would give Will Hardy a dynamic weapon that the current roster lacks. Peterson's powerful 6-foot-6 frame would allow him to play seamlessly alongside Keyonte George in a highly potent, multi-talented young backcourt, with both players capable of alternating between primary ball-handling and off-ball scoring roles. With Markkanen drawing defensive attention on the perimeter, Peterson would have immense space to attack the paint, exploit mid-range mismatches, and showcase the elite on-ball creation that was restricted during his collegiate career at Kansas.
CONCLUSION: TWO PATHS TO SUPERSTARDOM

In Dybantsa, franchises are looking at a hyper-efficient, historically dominant 6-foot-9 jumbo wing who possesses a flawless modern physical profile, elite scoring credentials, and the defensive tools to match. In Peterson, talent evaluators see an unmatched scoring virtuoso, a 6-foot-6 guard whose combination of pure speed, ball manipulation, and multi-level shot-making gives him the highest offensive ceiling in the entire class now that his collegiate health concerns are firmly in the rearview mirror.
